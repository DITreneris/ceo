Chapter 1Daily · Fast

Daily prompt — Fast

Daily · Fast5 min
ROLE:
You are an operations analyst advising a CEO/COO at a US B2B SaaS or services company.

CONTEXT:
- Company type: [SaaS / services / hybrid]
- Revenue model: [subscription / project / mixed]
- Current goal: [e.g. hit weekly revenue target, reduce churn]
- Decision horizon: Today (based on yesterday actuals)
- Constraints: [cash, headcount, key deadline]

FACTS:
- Revenue (yesterday): [$amount]
- New clients / leads: [count and names if known]
- Expenses (yesterday): [$amount]
- Key events: [contracts, incidents, hires, losses]

TASK:
What should I do differently today based on yesterday’s data?

REASONING RULES:
- Separate facts, assumptions, and recommendations.
- Do not invent missing numbers. Flag gaps before recommending action.
- Prioritize cash, customer impact, execution capacity, and reversibility.
- Max 3 priorities. For each recommendation, one-sentence business reason.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. Executive summary (max 5 lines)
2. Decision table: priority | why it matters | action | owner | deadline | risk
3. Risks and mitigations
4. Data needed before commitment
5. CEO decision required: [one line]

QUALITY GATE:
If input is too vague, ask up to 5 clarifying questions before the final answer.

Chapter 1Daily · Deep

Daily prompt — Deep

Daily · Deep15 min
ROLE:
You are an operations analyst advising a CEO/COO at a US B2B SaaS or services company.

CONTEXT:
- Company type: [SaaS / services / hybrid]
- Revenue model: [subscription / project / mixed]
- Current goal: [e.g. stabilize MRR, clear support backlog]
- Decision horizon: Today + knock-on effects this week
- Constraints: [cash, team capacity, customer commitments]

FACTS:
- Revenue (yesterday): [$amount]
- New clients / leads: [count and description]
- Expenses (yesterday): [$amount]
- Key events: [specific events with dates]
- Pipeline note (if relevant): [one line]

TASK:
Provide a deep daily operating review: what changed, why it matters, and what to do today.

REASONING RULES:
- Separate facts, assumptions, and recommendations.
- Do not invent missing numbers. Flag missing data before action.
- Use numbers from FACTS only; label estimates clearly.
- Max 3 priorities. Each action needs owner and deadline.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. Executive summary (max 5 lines)
2. Decision table: priority | rationale | action | owner | deadline | risk
3. Risks and mitigations (with leading indicators)
4. Data needed before commitment
5. CEO decision required: [specific yes/no or choice]

QUALITY GATE:
If FACTS lack revenue, expenses, or events, ask up to 5 clarifying questions first.

Chapter 2Weekly · Fast

Weekly prompt — Fast

Weekly · Fast10 min
ROLE:
You are a weekly operations analyst advising a CEO/COO.

CONTEXT:
- Company type: [SaaS / services]
- Decision horizon: This week
- Constraints: [cash runway months, hiring freeze, etc.]

FACTS:
- Weekly revenue: [$amount]
- Weekly costs: [$amount]
- Cash balance: [$amount]
- Active projects: [name, % complete, status]
- Sales pipeline: [deals, stages, dates]

TASK:
Which projects should be prioritized and what are the top 3 actions this week?

REASONING RULES:
- Do not invent facts. Flag missing pipeline or cash data.
- Prioritize by cash impact, customer risk, and execution capacity.
- Max 3 priorities. Prioritization criteria: impact × urgency × reversibility.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. Executive summary (max 5 lines)
2. Decision table: priority | rationale | action | owner | deadline | risk
3. Project priority ranking (1–3) with one-line why
4. Data needed before commitment
5. CEO decision required

QUALITY GATE:
Ask up to 5 clarifying questions if projects or pipeline are vague.

Weekly prompt — Deep

Weekly · Deep20 min
ROLE:
You are a weekly operations analyst advising a CEO/COO.

CONTEXT:
- Company type: [SaaS / services]

FACTS:

Full weekly operating review: priorities, resource trade-offs, and leadership actions.

3. Project trade-off note (what to delay and why)
4. Risks and mitigations
5. CEO decision required

If runway or pipeline missing, ask clarifying questions before final output.

Chapter 3Reflection

CEO reflection (weekly)

End the week with fact-based coaching. Challenge assumptions—not comfort.

ROLE:
You are a strategic coach for a CEO/COO.

CONTEXT:
- Decision horizon: End of this week → next week behavior
- Constraints: [time, team capacity]

FACTS:
- Weekly goal: [goal]
- What went well (facts only): [list]
- What went poorly (facts only): [list]
- Key metrics: [numbers]
- Assumptions I am making: [list]

TASK:
Run a fact-based weekly CEO reflection.

REASONING RULES:
- Separate facts from interpretation. Challenge unstated assumptions.
- Do not invent events or metrics.
- Recommend one behavior change for next week (specific, measurable).

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. Facts vs interpretation (table)
2. Assumption challenge (max 3)
3. One behavior change | owner | how to measure
4. CEO decision required

QUALITY GATE:
If facts are vague, ask up to 5 clarifying questions.

FinanceDiscipline

Economic discipline (top 5 rules)

  1. Cash first: Every weekly review names cash balance and runway trend.
  2. Runway trigger: If runway < 6 months, weekly CEO priority must include cash action.
  3. Cost spike: Any weekly cost >10% vs prior week gets CFO label: one-time vs recurring.
  4. Pipeline risk: If >50% pipeline in one deal, name contingency owner.
  5. No vanity priorities: AI cannot prioritize “brand awareness” without metric tie to revenue or retention.

QualityMistakes

Common mistakes (and fixes)

  • Skipping review step: Treating AI output as final → always mark facts vs assumptions.
  • Weekly data on Daily prompt: Blurs decisions → use correct prompt page.
  • No decision log: Same debates repeat → log owner and follow-up date.
  • Promo over practice: Spending more time on tools than 10-min ritual → run Day 1 of First 7 days first.

Quick ReferenceCEO Quick Picks

CEO quick picks

When you have 2 minutes — not 15. Copy, paste, get a decision.

Monday morning · 2 min

When: No time for Daily Deep, need priorities now.

Yesterday: Revenue $[X], Leads [N], Expenses $[X].
Give me 3 priorities for today with one owner each.
Flag if runway or a deal is at risk. Max 5 lines.

→ Output: 3 priorities · owners · one risk flag

Cash red flag · 2 min

When: Runway concern or unexpected cost spike.

Cash balance: $[X]. Runway: [N] months.
If runway < 6 months, name ONE cash action this week.
Owner and deadline required.

→ Output: one action · owner · deadline

Priority cap · 2 min

When: Team has more than 5 AI-generated bullets.

Here are my current priorities: [list].
Force exactly 3. Drop or merge the rest.
State the one-line reason for each kept priority.

→ Output: 3 priorities · clear rationale

Friday reflection · 2 min

When: End of week, 5 minutes before close.

This week: [2-3 facts — what went well, what missed].
Give me: 3 facts vs 1 assumption I'm making.
Recommend one behavior change for next week.

→ Output: facts · assumption · one behavior change

Escalate to strategy · 2 min

When: Ops issue needs board or 90-day context — hand off to Strategy Playbook.

Issue: [one sentence]. Urgency: [high/medium].
Is this a daily ops decision or a strategic commitment?
If strategic, name the framework: runway / growth lever / risk.

→ Output: ops or strategic label · next step · which Strategy Playbook prompt to use
